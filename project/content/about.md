# Why I started High Fees Not For Me

I'm Mike: a Tampa diner, IT guy, and frequent traveler who got tired of this.

Over the past few years, on the road and at home, I started seeing the same thing everywhere I went:
the price I was quoted wasn't the price I paid. Hotels, restaurants, entertainment — there was always
something extra waiting on the final bill.

What finally got to me was here at home in Tampa. At restaurant after restaurant, especially the
higher-end ones, the check came back with charges I hadn't agreed to: a mandatory gratuity, a
"facility fee," a "back of the house tip." When I asked about one, I was told it was "our new policy."
It stayed on the bill.

## How it spreads

Hotel resort fees are widely traced to Las Vegas, and they spread from there to hotels across the
country. Restaurants seem to be following the same path: one place adds a fee, nobody walks out,
and soon the places around it are doing the same. Each fee is small. Together, they mean the menu
price isn't the real price anymore.

In many other countries, the price you see has to be the price you pay. In the US, it often isn't.
Federal rules now require hotels and ticket sellers to show the full price up front, but restaurants
aren't covered. [VERIFY: FTC Rule on Unfair or Deceptive Fees, effective 2025-05-12.]

## What I want

Simple: **the price should include everything.** If a business needs to charge more to pay its staff
a fair wage, put it in the menu price and let customers decide. Don't add it after the meal.

## What this site does

High Fees Not For Me collects real fees that real customers ran into, starting in Florida. Every
listing is backed by a receipt or an official source, and I review each one personally before it's
published. We report what the receipt shows; businesses can dispute any listing.

If you've been hit with a fee you didn't expect, [report it](/report). The more we document, the
harder these fees are to hide.
